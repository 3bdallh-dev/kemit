Words of the Gods

The word "hieroglyph" comes from the Greek hieros (sacred) and gluphe (carving). To the Egyptians, these symbols were mdw nṯr — "words of the god." Hieroglyphs were used primarily for religious and monumental inscriptions, while hieratic and demotic scripts served everyday purposes.

For nearly 1,500 years, the meaning of hieroglyphs was lost to history. Then, in 1799, the Rosetta Stone was discovered — a trilingual decree that provided the key. In 1822, Jean-François Champollion cracked the code, unlocking the voices of the pharaohs.

Three Scripts, One Language

𓂋

Hieroglyphic

The formal script used on monuments and temples. Over 700 distinct signs combining logographic and alphabetic elements.

𓏏

Hieratic

A cursive form of hieroglyphs used by priests and scribes for religious texts and administrative documents on papyrus.

𓆓

Demotic

The everyday script used by the common people. Even more cursive than hieratic, it became the dominant script in the Late Period.
