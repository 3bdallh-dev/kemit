The Ages of Egypt

From the dawn of civilization to the modern discovery of its greatest secrets.

3100 BCE

Unification of Upper & Lower Egypt under King Narmer. The First Dynasty begins, marking the start of recorded Egyptian history and the age of the pharaohs.

2580 BCE

Construction of the Great Pyramid of Giza under Pharaoh Khufu. The oldest and largest of the three pyramids, it remains one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World.

1550 BCE

The New Kingdom begins — Egypt's imperial age. Pharaohs like Hatshepsut, Thutmose III, and Akhenaten expand the empire from Nubia to Syria.

1353 BCE

Akhenaten's religious revolution. The pharaoh abandons traditional gods for the worship of Aten, the sun disk. His wife Nefertiti becomes the most famous queen in history.

1323 BCE

Death and burial of Tutankhamun in the Valley of the Kings. The boy king's tomb would remain undisturbed for over 3,000 years, preserving its treasures intact.

1279 BCE

Ramses II ascends the throne. Known as Ramses the Great, he builds Abu Simbel, fights the Hittites at Kadesh, and rules for 66 years — the longest reign in Egyptian history.

332 BCE

Alexander the Great conquers Egypt, ending Pharaonic rule. The Ptolemaic Dynasty begins, blending Greek and Egyptian culture. Alexandria becomes the world's greatest city.

30 BCE

Death of Cleopatra VII marks the end of ancient Egypt. The kingdom becomes a Roman province, closing the curtain on three millennia of pharaonic civilization.

1799 CE

The Rosetta Stone is discovered by French soldiers near Rashid (Rosetta). This trilingual inscription becomes the key to deciphering hieroglyphs, unlocking Egypt's written history.

1922 CE

Howard Carter discovers Tutankhamun's intact tomb in the Valley of the Kings. The world is stunned by the treasure within, sparking a global fascination with ancient Egypt.

1902 CE

The Egyptian Museum opens its doors in Tahrir Square, Cairo. Designed by French architect Marcel Dourgnon, it becomes the world's greatest repository of Pharaonic antiquities.
